# 12-Week Plan: Faster Serve, Quicker Feet
**Sept 7 – Nov 29, 2026 | Nationals Nov 30 | Men's 40s Doubles | 20 min/day**

---

## The Strategy

You have 140 sessions of 20 minutes. That's 47 hours — enough to change real things, but only if every session has one job. So each day has one job.

**Two targets, two mechanisms:**

- **Serve speed** at 42 rarely comes from getting stronger. It comes from *sequencing* — legs drive, hips rotate, shoulders lag behind hips, then the arm whips through. Most 40s players leak power because the shoulders open with the hips instead of after them. Medicine ball throws train that lag better than anything in a gym.
- **Movement speed** in doubles isn't a 40-yard dash. It's first-step explosiveness off a split step, lateral push-off for the poach, and the drop-step retreat for the lob. All three are *ankle and hip stiffness* problems — how fast you can absorb and redirect force, not how much force you can make.

**Three blocks:**

| Block | Weeks | Dates | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Build the chassis** | 1–4 | Sep 7 – Oct 4 | Tissue tolerance, mobility, clean movement patterns. Moderate intent. |
| **2 — Build the engine** | 5–8 | Oct 5 – Nov 1 | Max-intent power. Heaviest throws, hardest plyos, serve velocity work. |
| **3 — Sharpen** | 9–12 | Nov 2 – Nov 29 | Volume drops, speed stays. Fresh legs by match day. |

**The one rule that makes this work:** power days are *quality* days. Every throw and every jump is 100% intent with full rest between reps. If you're breathing hard on a power day, you're doing it wrong. Save the fatigue for Saturdays.

---

## Equipment

- **Medicine ball, 4–6 lb (2–3 kg).** Non-bouncing "slam" style. This is the single most important purchase. Light is correct — heavy balls slow the throw down and kill the transfer.
- **Resistance band**, light-to-medium loop or tube.
- **Jump rope.**
- **Your racket** + a racket cover or a second racket for weighted shadow swings.
- **A wall you can throw at** — garage, fence, backstop. Concrete or a fence panel.
- Optional but high-value: **a pocket radar or phone app** to actually measure serve speed monthly, and a phone on slow-mo (240fps) for video.

# The Drill Library

Read this once. After that, the weekly calendars just tell you sets and reps.

## Serve A — Rotation (Mon)

**Warm-up (4 min)**
- Band external rotation, 2×15 each arm. Elbow pinned to ribs, slow.
- Band pull-aparts, 2×15.
- Thoracic open-books, 10/side, lying on your side, knees stacked, follow your hand with your eyes.
- 20 easy wrist snaps with the racket — just the last 6 inches of the serve.

**Main work**
1. **Rotational wall throw (side toss).** Stand side-on to the wall, feet just wider than shoulders, ball at hip. Load into the back hip, then fire: back hip turns *first*, chest follows, arms last. Throw hard enough that catching the rebound is annoying. Full reset between reps. *This is your #1 serve exercise.*
2. **Step-behind rotational throw.** Same throw, but take a shuffle step into it first — this is closer to the serve's momentum transfer.
3. **Overhead slam, half-kneeling.** Tall-kneeling on one knee, ball overhead, slam it down in front. Trains the abdominal snap that finishes the serve. Keep the ribs down, don't arch the low back.
4. **Weighted shadow serve.** Racket with a cover on it, or two rackets. Slow the trophy position, feel the shoulders stay *closed* while the hips open, then accelerate. 8 reps, then 4 reps with a normal racket at full speed to "wake up" the pattern.

**Finish (2 min)** — Cross-body shoulder stretch 30s/side, sleeper stretch 30s/side.

## Serve B — Leg Drive (Thu)

**Warm-up (4 min)**
- Ankle rockers against a wall, 10/side.
- World's greatest stretch, 5/side.
- 30 seconds easy jump rope.

**Main work**
1. **Serve-stance jump.** Set up in your exact serve stance. Load, then jump *up and forward-left* (right-hander) — the direction you actually leave the ground on serve. Land soft, reset fully. Not for height; for direction.
2. **Scoop toss.** Ball between your feet, squat down, then explode up and throw the ball up and behind you as high as you can. Full triple extension. This is leg drive in one movement.
3. **Back-leg loading — split squat.** Rear-foot-elevated split squat, slow down (3 sec), fast up. Your back leg on the serve is the spring.
4. **Pronation snaps.** Elbow bent 90°, upper arm supported on your other hand or a table, light band or 2–3 lb weight. Rotate the forearm from palm-up to palm-down fast, control back slow. Racket-head speed lives in the last 40 milliseconds and this is what does it.

**Finish (2 min)** — Wrist flexor stretch, forearm shake-out, 30s each.

## Move A — First Step (Tue)

**Warm-up (4 min)**
- Pogo hops, 3×20 — stiff ankles, minimal knee bend, feel the bounce off the floor. This *is* your movement training as much as the main work.
- Leg swings, 10 each direction each leg.

**Main work**
1. **Split-step → lateral push-off.** Split step, land, and immediately drive off the outside foot 3 hard strides to the side. Alternate directions. The cue: *land already loaded*, don't land then think.
2. **Skater bounds.** Lateral bound, land on one leg, **stick it for one full second** before the next. The stick is the point — that's your deceleration into a volley.
3. **Crossover step start.** From a ready position, crossover step and accelerate 3 strides. This beats a shuffle for anything more than a step away — it's the poach and the wide return.
4. **Wall shuffle.** Hands on a wall at a lean, rapid alternating foot drive. Pure turnover speed.

**Finish (2 min)** — Copenhagen plank (side plank with top leg on a chair), or a knee-supported version. 20–30s/side. Groin insurance for lunging volleys.

## Move B — React & Decelerate (Fri)

**Warm-up (4 min)**
- Jump rope 60s, A-skips 2×15, hip openers 5/side.

**Main work**
1. **Mini 5-10-5.** Set two markers 5 yards apart, start in the middle. Sprint one way, touch, all the way back, touch, back to middle. Two reps, then rest a full minute. Brutal, brief, exactly the shape of a doubles point.
2. **Drop-step retreat.** From ready position, drop-step back, take 3 crossover strides retreating, then jump and shadow-swing an overhead. Both directions. *This is the shot that ends 40s doubles matches.*
3. **Deceleration drops.** 4 hard strides forward, then stop dead and hold a low athletic position for 2 seconds. Braking ability is what lets you attack a short ball without recovering late.
4. **Ball drop reaction.** Drop a ball from shoulder height, let it bounce once, catch it. Each rep, start a step farther away. Or do it against a wall with a partner. If solo: toss a ball at a wall and react to the rebound.

**Finish (2 min)** — Calf and hip flexor stretch, 30s each side.

## Strength (Sat)

Straight through, minimal rest, ~14 min of work.

1. **Eccentric heel raises.** Up on two feet, down on one, 3 seconds down. This is the single best Achilles and calf-tear prevention there is, and it's non-negotiable at 42.
2. **Split squat** (bodyweight or holding anything heavy).
3. **Single-leg RDL** — hinge at the hip, back leg straight behind, touch the floor. Hamstring and glute, plus balance.
4. **Pallof press / anti-rotation hold** — band anchored at chest height to your side, press out and hold. Your torso's ability to resist rotation is what lets you *transmit* rotation on the serve.
5. **Push-up variation** — feet elevated, or slow tempo.

## Mobility & Prehab (Wed)

Move slowly. 20 minutes, no rushing.

- **Thoracic rotation, quadruped** — hand behind head, rotate open. 10/side. Serve speed is capped by how much your mid-back can turn.
- **90/90 hip switches** — 15 slow switches.
- **Couch stretch** (rear foot on a couch or wall) — 60s/side. Hip flexor length = leg drive range.
- **Shoulder CARs** — slowest possible full circle, 5/side.
- **Sleeper stretch + cross-body** — 45s/side.
- **Band shoulder series** — external rotation, internal rotation, scaption, 2×15 each.
- **Dead hang** from a bar, 2×30s if you have one.

## Recovery (Sun)

- 12–15 min easy walk, bike, or swim. Nasal breathing only — you should be able to hold a conversation.
- Foam roll or ball: calves, quads, lats, upper back. 5 min.
- Band external rotation 2×15 — cuff work every single Sunday, forever.
- **5 minutes of visualization.** Sit, eyes closed, and play points. Your serve going where you aimed. The poach you took. Reading the return. This is not filler; imagery has real transfer for skill and for nerves.

## Testing — Three Dates

Do these on **Oct 4, Nov 1, and Nov 22.** Same time of day, same warm-up, write it down.

| Test | How | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Serve speed** | 10 first serves, radar or app. Record fastest and average. | The actual goal |
| **First-serve %** | Same 10 serves — how many in? | Speed you can't land is worth nothing in doubles |
| **5-10-5 time** | Best of 2, phone timer or a partner | Change of direction |
| **Broad jump** | Standing, best of 3, measure in feet | Whole-body power proxy |
| **Lateral bound** | Single-leg, distance, each side | Reveals side-to-side imbalance |
| **Video** | 240fps slow-mo of 3 serves, from behind and from the side | The most useful thing on this list |

Realistic expectation over 12 weeks at 42: **3–7 mph on the serve** if your mechanics are already decent, more if the video reveals a sequencing fault you can fix. Movement improvements will feel bigger than they measure — a tenth of a second on the 5-10-5 is the difference between reaching a poach and watching it.

## Six Things That Matter More Than the Program

1. **Get the video looked at.** One 240fps clip of your serve, reviewed by a good coach, could be worth more than all 47 hours here. The most common 40s fault is the shoulders opening with the hips — costs 8–10 mph and is fixable in weeks.

2. **Toss consistency is a speed multiplier.** An inconsistent toss forces you to decelerate mid-swing to make contact. Fixing the toss often "adds" 4 mph that was always there.

3. **Never do power work on a sore shoulder.** If the cuff is cranky, swap that day's session for the mobility day and move on. One tendon flare-up in October costs you the whole block.

4. **Sleep is the actual training variable.** At 42, adaptation happens overnight or it doesn't happen. Seven hours minimum on the nights after Block 2 power days.

5. **In doubles, the return is the shot that decides matches** — more than the serve. If you get a second 20 minutes some weeks, spend it on return-of-serve blocking and first-volley reflexes rather than more serve work.

6. **Protein.** 100–140g a day. You're asking a 42-year-old body to build power on 20 minutes a day; don't under-fuel the recovery.

---

## If You Miss a Day

Don't make it up. Skip it and do the next day's session on schedule. The calendar is a rhythm, not a debt. Missing one day costs nothing. Trying to cram two sessions into one is how people get hurt in October and watch nationals from a chair.
